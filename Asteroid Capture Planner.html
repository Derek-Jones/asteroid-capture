Find near-Earth asteroids a single fully fuelled Starship could steer into an Earth orbit with apogee inside 100,000 miles (160,934 km). Pick an asteroid to see its mass, the burn plan, the flight time and the impact corridor if targeting goes wrong.

Catalogue

Grey entries are excluded: faster than once per hour, or faster than once per day for asteroids 200 m or larger. Close-pass dates for grey entries are two-body estimates and can be off by a lunar distance or more. Select one to enter a measured rotation period.

    Add asteroids

    Paste JPL SBDB CSV (header with full_name,a,e,i,om,w,ma,epoch,diameter,rot_per,H; epoch as JD) or raw astorb.dat lines from asteroid.lowell.edu/astorb. astorb has no rotation periods, so add one at the end of a line as | 36.5 (hours) or the asteroid is excluded.

    Starship and model settings

    Starship Block 4 ship: 2,300 t propellant (planned) and 380 s vacuum Isp, the best figures in Wikipedia's Starship infobox; 15,828 kN listed maximum ship thrust. Block 4 dry mass is not listed, so 100 t (Block 1 figure) is assumed. Density: mean of the five spacecraft-measured near-Earth asteroids (Eros 2.67, Didymos 2.4, Itokawa 1.90, Ryugu 1.19, Bennu 1.19); bounds are the extremes. Despin assumes the engines gimbal 15° (typical Raptor range) and push from the ship's tail, about 52 m (Block 2 ship height; Block 4 is not listed) beyond the asteroid's surface. Changes recalculate every asteroid.
    Method and limits

    Mass. m = ρ·πd³/6. Diameters come from radar or NEOWISE where published; otherwise from H with albedo 0.05–0.35. The low bound pairs the smallest diameter with ρ = 1.19 g/cm³, the high bound the largest diameter with 2.67 g/cm³.

    Despin. Asteroids under 200 m may spin as fast as once per hour; larger ones must turn slower than once per day. An unknown rotation period is assumed to be 1.05 h (63 min), slightly slower than once per hour, so unmeasured asteroids under 200 m are planned with that spin. Every candidate is despun first: the spin's angular momentum L = 0.4·M·R²·ω is cancelled by gimballed thrust, using propellant m = L/(ve·sin δ·(R + ship length)). That propellant is subtracted before the orbital burns. The sideways push during despin is ignored, and tumblers are treated as a single spin at their main period.

    Propulsion. Δv available = Isp·g₀·ln((M + m_dry + m_prop)/(M + m_dry)), with the asteroid, ship and propellant as one rigid body. Burns last minutes, so they are treated as impulses.

    Orbits. Osculating elements from Lowell's astorb.dat (epoch 2026-09-17), propagated as two-body Kepler orbits. The catalogue holds the slow rotators with measured periods plus the 115 near-Earth asteroids that astorb's orbits bring within 0.02 AU (7.8 lunar distances) of Earth between November 2026 and November 2036, among objects observed for at least 30 days. Planetary perturbations are ignored, so pass distances can be off by a lunar distance or more; Apophis's 2029 pass uses the published 38,017 km. This is a screening tool, not an ephemeris.

    Burn 1, targeting. Moves the natural flyby from impact parameter b₀ to the b that gives the chosen perigee, b = r_p·√(1 + 2μ/(r_p·v∞²)). Lever arm: along-track drift Δs ≈ 3·Δv·t, projected onto the B-plane.

    Burn 3, capture. At perigee, slows the asteroid from √(v∞² + 2μ/r_p) to the speed of an ellipse with apogee 160,934 km. Perigee is optimised between 500 km altitude and 160,934 km. Burn 2 is a 5% reserve for course correction.

    Impact corridor. An over- or under-performing Burn 1 slides the flyby along the line through the B-plane. Those trajectories are integrated through Earth's gravity to 100 km altitude and mapped on the rotating Earth. If only the capture burn fails, the asteroid passes at the targeted perigee and leaves; it cannot hit Earth.

    Not modelled. Lunar gravity assists (which can cut capture Δv a lot), solar and planetary perturbations, Yarkovsky drift, and the long-term stability of a 0.42-lunar-distance orbit under lunar perturbation.
